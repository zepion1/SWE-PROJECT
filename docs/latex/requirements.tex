\section{1. Introduction}\label{introduction}

\subsection{1.1. Purpose}\label{purpose}

This document defines the functional and non-functional requirements of the AI-Driven Pharmaceutical Real-Time Inventory and Customer Information System, along with high-level specifications describing its expected behavior. It serves as a reference for the development team and stakeholders throughout system design, implementation, and testing, ensuring a shared understanding of the system\textquotesingle s intended functionality and constraints.

\subsection{1.2. Scope}\label{scope}

The system will cover inventory management operations for participating pharmacies, including product and supplier records, stock tracking, expiration management, purchase orders, and reporting. It will also include AI-based demand forecasting and inventory recommendations, as well as a customer-facing portal for accessing medication availability, pricing, and pharmacy locations.

The system will support inventory updates based on sales recorded at the pharmacy\textquotesingle s point-of-sale system. Inventory decisions involving stock adjustments and purchase orders will remain subject to approval by authorized pharmacy personnel.

Out of scope for this version are prescription dispensing and e-prescribing, insurance claim processing, online payment, home delivery, and any medical or dosing advice.

\section{2. Overall Description}\label{overall-description}

\subsection{2.1. System Overview}\label{system-overview}

The system will operate as a centralized platform connecting multiple participating pharmacies through a shared application. Each pharmacy will maintain its own inventory records, while the platform will provide a unified way to access and manage information across locations.

The application will have two main views: one for pharmacy operations and another for customers. The pharmacy view will provide role-based access to operational functions, while the customer view will allow public access to product information with optional account registration for additional features.

Inventory information will be updated through staff actions and sales recorded at the pharmacy\textquotesingle s point-of-sale system. Machine learning models will analyze historical sales data to generate recommendations for pharmacy personnel to review.

The system will distinguish between staff roles, customers, and system administrators, with their respective responsibilities and permissions described in \textbf{Table 2.1.1.}

\multicolumn{3}{|>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}m{(\linewidth - 4\tabcolsep) * \real{1.0000} + 4\tabcolsep}|}{%
\textbf{User Management and Security}} \\ \hline
F1 & Staff sign-in and roles & Staff members will sign in with a username, a password and a second proof of identity, such as a one-time code. Each person then will only see the screens and actions their role allows (Section 2). The System Administrator will create staff member accounts and assign them roles. \\ \hline
F2 & Customer access and accounts & Customers will be able to search and view results without creating an account. A customer who chooses to register will gain extra features, such as back-in-stock notices (F23). \\ \hline
F3 & Record of changes (audit log) & Every change to inventory will be recorded automatically, including who made it, when, and what changed, so any change will be able to be traced afterward. \\ \hline
\multicolumn{3}{|>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}m{(\linewidth - 4\tabcolsep) * \real{1.0000} + 4\tabcolsep}|}{%
\textbf{Inventory Management}} \\ \hline
F4 & Product records & Staff members will be able to add a product, change its details, or deactivate it. Each product will have a name, National Drug Code (NDC), strength, dosage form, category, and price. \\ \hline
F5 & Receiving stock & When a delivery arrives, staff members will scan each item's barcode or type its details by hand. The system will record the lot number, expiration date, quantity, supplier, and add the quantity to stock on hand. \\ \hline
F6 & Sales and stock corrections & Each sale recorded at the pharmacy's point-of-sale system (the checkout) will reduce stock on hand automatically. Staff will also be able to correct a quantity by hand and in doing so must select a reason. Manual corrections will take effect only when a Pharmacist/Manager approves them (Section 2). \\ \hline
F7 & Stock on hand & For every product, staff will be able to see the quantity currently on hand broken down by lot, search, and filter the list. Receipts, sales, and corrections will show up within 5 seconds (Section 3). \\ \hline
F8 & Physical counts & During a physical count (cycle count), staff members will enter the quantity they counted. The system will compare it with the recorded quantity and flag any difference for a Pharmacist/Manager to review. \\ \hline
\multicolumn{3}{|>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}m{(\linewidth - 4\tabcolsep) * \real{1.0000} + 4\tabcolsep}|}{%
\textbf{Expiration Management}} \\ \hline
F9 & Expiration tracking & The system will keep the expiration date of every lot and show a dashboard of items that have expired or will expire within 30, 60, or 90 days. \\ \hline
F10 & Expiration alerts and sale block & Once a day, the system will alert the Pharmacist/Manager and the Technician about expired and soon-to-expire items. Expired stock won't be able to be sold: the system will block the sale. \\ \hline
F11 & Use-first advice and disposal records & When a product has more than one lot on hand, the system will recommend using the lot that expires first. When expired items are disposed of, or when products are returned to the supplier, staff members will record it and the system will calculate the lost value in dollars. \\ \hline
\multicolumn{3}{|>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}m{(\linewidth - 4\tabcolsep) * \real{1.0000} + 4\tabcolsep}|}{%
\textbf{Reordering and Suppliers}} \\ \hline
F12 & Supplier records and reorder points & Staff members will keep a record for each supplier (contact details, delivery lead time, minimum order, prices) and set a reorder point for each product: the stock level at which more should be ordered. \\ \hline
F13 & Reorder alerts and draft orders & When a product's stock falls to its reorder point, the system will alert staff members and prepare a draft purchase order. A draft will go no further until a Pharmacist/Manager approves it. \\ \hline
F14 & Sending and tracking orders & An approved order will be sent to the supplier. Staff members will be able to follow each order's status from Draft through to Received. When the delivery arrives, the system will flag any difference between what was ordered and what was received. \\ \hline
F15 & Expiration-risk and unusual-activity warnings & The system will point out lots that are likely to expire before they are sold and inventory activity that looks unusual. Each warning or recommendation will come with an explanation of why it was raised. \\ \hline
\multicolumn{3}{|>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}m{(\linewidth - 4\tabcolsep) * \real{1.0000} + 4\tabcolsep}|}{%
\textbf{AI Chatbot and Machine Learning Analytics}} \\ \hline
F16 & Demand forecasts & For each product, the system will estimate how much will be sold over the next 7, 30, and 90 days, based on past sales, the time of year, and trends. \\ \hline
F17 & Reorder recommendations & The system will recommend a reorder point and an order quantity for each product. The pharmacist will be able to accept, change, or reject each recommendation. \\ \hline
F18 & Human approval of AI output & The AI will only advise. No order will be placed and no stock record will be changed based on the AI output, until a person approves it. \\ \hline
\multicolumn{3}{|>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}m{(\linewidth - 4\tabcolsep) * \real{1.0000} + 4\tabcolsep}|}{%
\textbf{Customer Information Portal}} \\ \hline
F19 & Product search and availability & A customer will be able to search by brand name, generic name, or NDC. Each result will show the status as In Stock, Low Stock, or Out of Stock. Exact quantities will never be shown to the public. \\ \hline
F20 & Price display & Each result will show the retail (cash) price, with a note that the price with insurance may differ. \\ \hline
F21 & Nearest pharmacy & Using the customer's device location (with consent) or a ZIP code they may enter, the system will list the nearest participating pharmacies that have the item in stock, closest first, with a map and directions. The system will never store the customer's location. \\ \hline
F22 & Controlled substances & For Schedule II controlled substances, availability will be hidden, and the customer will see ``Contact pharmacy for availability.'' \\ \hline
F23 & Generic alternatives and back-in-stock notices & Results will show generic alternatives where they exist. A registered customer will be able to request notifications when an out-of-stock item is available again. \\ \hline
\multicolumn{3}{|>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}m{(\linewidth - 4\tabcolsep) * \real{1.0000} + 4\tabcolsep}|}{%
\textbf{Dashboards and Reporting}} \\ \hline
F24 & Pharmacist dashboard & A single screen will summarize low-stock items, expiring items, pending orders, and AI alerts. \\ \hline
F25 & Reports & Staff will be able to produce reports on inventory value, stock movement, expiration losses, forecast accuracy, and export them for use outside the system. \\ \hline
